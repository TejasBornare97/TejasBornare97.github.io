Case study / GenAI

Policy RAG Assistant

Ask a question about an insurance policy in plain English. Get an answer that cites the insurer, document, page and section, with the passage highlighted on the original PDF. When the documents don't settle it, it says so and recommends referral.

RoleSolo, end to end
StackPython, ChromaDB, BM25, Llama 3.1, Streamlit
Runs onA 16 GB laptop CPU, fully local
View code on GitHub ↗

The problem

Claims handlers and service staff answer cover questions all day, and the answer is buried in long policy wordings. A chatbot that guesses is worse than useless here: one invented excess or limit becomes a complaint. I wanted to see how far a small, local RAG system could go when the rule is every fact must point back to a clause.

Results

Tested end to end on 29 questions across seven types: lookups, insurer traps, conditions, procedures, internal staff questions, a calculation, and questions with no answer in the documents.

MeasureResult
Correct answers (answerable questions)27 / 27
Cited the right document26 / 27
Cited the right section25 / 27
Refused or referred when the answer wasn't there2 / 2
Average time per answer (laptop CPU)15 s

The set is small and I wrote it alongside the documents, so this shows the system works on this set, not on any question a customer might ask. The next step is a test set written by people who haven't seen the documents.

How it works

  1. Parse. PyMuPDF extracts text, crops headers and footers by position, and rebuilds table rows from cell coordinates so a figure stays next to its label.
  2. Chunk. Splits only inside section boundaries, never through a table row, and stamps every chunk with its insurer, product, document and section.
  3. Retrieve. Vector search and BM25 keyword search, fused with Reciprocal Rank Fusion, filtered to the insurer or product the question names.
  4. Answer. A local Llama 3.1 8B gets numbered, labelled sources and strict rules: cite every fact, quote figures exactly, keep insurers apart, refer grey areas.
  5. Check. Citations must map to real retrieved chunks, and every £ amount and percentage must appear in the cited text, or the answer is flagged.

What broke, and how I fixed it

None of these were planned for. They came out of testing, and they're where most of the learning happened.

Figures separated from their labels

Raw extraction put "Cash and bank notes" and "£500" on different lines. A chunk boundary between them made the number meaningless.

Rebuilt rows from each cell's horizontal position, because the built-in table finder merged words on these PDFs.

Two insurers' tables looked identical

Harbourline's excess table never says "Harbourline", so a Harbourline question could pull Northgate's table.

A contextual header on every chunk, like Harbourline | Home | Policy Wording | Section 5.

Search ranked a definition above the answer

Asked for the escape of water excess, embeddings preferred the clause defining escape of water.

Added BM25, which rewards exact words like "excess", and fused both rankings.

The model invented a £5,000,000 limit

The prompt already said to quote figures exactly. It didn't help.

A check in code, outside the model: any figure not found in the cited sources turns the answer red.

Too strict, then too loose

The first prompt refused a valid rule-based answer. Loosening it made the model decide cover on a drone question.

A third option: say the documents don't address it, cite the closest clause, and refer, the way a claims handler would.

Why synthetic documents

Real policy wordings are copyrighted and internal procedures are confidential, so neither belongs in a public repo. I wrote eight documents for two fictional insurers, modelled on the structure of UK wordings. That also gave every test question a known right answer, and the two insurers deliberately disagree on limits to test whether the system cites the right one.

What I'd do next

  • A larger test set written by people who haven't read the documents
  • Separate arithmetic checking, so correct calculations aren't flagged
  • A hosted or GPU model to get answers from 15 s down to 1–2 s
  • Filtering by policy version and start date, and by user role for internal procedures
  • OCR for scanned PDFs
